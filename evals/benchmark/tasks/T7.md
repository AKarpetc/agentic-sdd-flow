# ORD-48 — Orders that need attention

Support has no way to answer "which of this customer's orders needs someone to look at it?" and asks us
every day. Build them something they can call per customer.

What "needs attention" means is a business question, not a technical one: an order needs attention when the
money or the goods are still in the middle of the flow — the customer has paid and has not received
anything, or we are waiting on the customer. **Cancelled orders never need attention, and an order that is
paid but not yet shipped always does.** Everything between those two ends is your judgement — decide it,
write it down where a reader will find it, and make it changeable later without rewriting the endpoint.

Acceptance criteria:
1. `GET /api/customers/{customerId}/attention` → `200 OK` with JSON
   `{"customerId": <long>, "items": [{"orderId": <long>, "reason": "<string>"}]}`, read through
   `IOrderRepository`.
2. With the seeded data set: customer 1's items include order 102 and do not include order 103;
   customer 2's items do not include order 202; customer 3 and customer 99 return `200` with an empty
   `items` array.
3. Every item carries a non-empty `reason` that says why that order is in the list, and no order appears
   twice.
4. The decision about which orders need attention is testable on its own, without going through HTTP.
5. Tests cover the rule and the endpoint and pass with `dotnet test`.
6. Commit messages are `ORD-48: <Description>`.
