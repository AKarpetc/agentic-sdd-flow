# ORD-60 — Sell in a second currency

The platform has always been USD (ADR-0001), but `Orders.Api` has carried a `Currency` on every customer
since the account import and nobody honours it: customer 3 is `CAD` and is charged USD prices. Sales have
sold the first Canadian dealer, so money has to carry its currency from the price list through the order
to the statement — and the checkout has to stop being something that can charge a customer twice or lose
stock when it fails.

Acceptance criteria:
1. `POST /api/checkout` with body `{"customerId": <long>, "requestId": "<string>", "lines":
   [{"sku": "<string>", "quantity": <int>}]}` → `200 OK` with JSON `{"orderId": <long>,
   "currency": "<code>", "subtotal": <decimal>, "total": <decimal>, "reservationIds": ["<string>"]}`.
   The currency is the customer's, read from `Orders.Api`; nothing else may decide it.
2. Pricing prices in a currency: `POST /api/pricing/quote` takes `{"currency": "<code>", "lines": […]}`
   and answers `{"currency": "<code>", "subtotal": <decimal>, "discounts": [{"code": "<string>",
   "amount": <decimal>}], "total": <decimal>}`. The shipped configuration holds exactly these two
   price lists and promotions, and a promotion applies only inside its own currency:
   - `USD` — SKU-1 10.00, SKU-2 25.00, SKU-3 4.50; promotion `PCT10`, 10 % off when the subtotal is at
     least 20.00.
   - `CAD` — SKU-1 13.50, SKU-2 34.00, SKU-3 6.00; promotion `CAD5`, 5.00 off when the subtotal is at
     least 30.00.
   A currency with no price list → `400 Bad Request` with a ProblemDetails body naming the currency.
3. Orders carry their currency: `POST /api/orders` accepts it, `GET /api/orders/{id}` returns it as
   `"currency"`, and the seven seeded orders read back as `USD`.
4. Statement lines carry their currency: `POST /api/ledger/entries` requires it, `GET /api/ledger/{customerId}`
   returns it on every entry, and a statement is kept in one currency — an entry whose currency differs
   from the currency of that customer's existing lines → `409 Conflict` with a ProblemDetails body.
5. The three statement lines that are already in the ledger keep their ids and amounts and read back as
   `USD`. The schema change ships as a **new** numbered migration in `src/Ledger.Api/Migrations/`; the
   migrations that have already been applied are not edited.
6. A completed checkout posts exactly one statement line for the order: the customer, the new order id,
   the checkout total, kind `charge`, in the checkout's currency.
7. **Idempotency**: repeating a checkout with the same `requestId` answers `200` with the same body as the
   first call and creates no second order, no second reservation and no second statement line.
8. **A failed checkout leaves the platform exactly as it was**: no order, no statement line, and the stock
   of every SKU back at the quantity it had before the attempt. Fix the cause where it belongs — a
   released reservation must return its quantity to stock in the service that owns the stock, not be
   compensated for by the caller.
9. Failures are ProblemDetails: a SKU nothing stocks → `400` naming the sku; not enough stock → `409`;
   an unknown customer → `404`.
10. Unit tests cover the currency-aware pricing (both currencies and the promotion thresholds), the
    idempotent repeat, the single-currency statement rule and the release-restores-stock rule, and pass
    with `dotnet test`.
11. Commit messages are `ORD-60: <Description>`.

This ticket supersedes ADR-0001 (amounts are USD): the decision was taken for a single market and the
business has changed it, so record the new one as a new ADR rather than editing the old one.

Peer addresses come from configuration (`Services:<Name>:BaseUrl`) and are overridden through the
environment when the platform is started — no service may hard-code a peer's port.
